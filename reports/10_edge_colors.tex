\textbf{The graph.} Every node that sits in two or more subgraphs is replaced by one copy per
subgraph. Each copy keeps all of the node's edges. A \emph{coloring} sorts the edges into groups:
inside edges (both ends in one subgraph), outside edges, sibling edges (between two copies of one
node), and our clique. Each group gets its own weights in the GNN.

\textbf{What we ran}, in four steps, all on GLASS's splits:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item 23 colorings, each with our training and with GLASS's training, 3 seeds. Two colorings
        have no copies: the plain graph, and the plain graph split into inside/outside edges.
  \item The 3 best colorings $\times$ 4 aggregations $\times$ 7 poolings, 3 seeds.
  \item The 3 best setups $\times$ a small grid (hidden size, lr, dropout, edge counts), 3 seeds.
  \item The 3 finalists and our previous best model, 10 seeds.
\end{enumerate}
em\_user, hpo\_metab and hpo\_neuro ran without copies only. With copies, their graphs have 35M to
1.3B edges and do not fit in GPU memory.

\section*{Findings}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Better on 5 of 8 datasets.} The largest gains are on ppi\_bp (+3.6) and coreness
        (+3.0). hpo\_neuro (69.6) is above both GLASS (68.5) and GPEN (69.1), and hpo\_metab (61.1) is
        0.3 below it.
  \item \textbf{Worse on cut\_ratio ($-4.6$) and em\_user ($-7.7$).} em\_user varies a lot
        between seeds ($\pm$6 to $\pm$9).
  \item \textbf{Copies help only on the synthetic datasets.} In step 1, test with no copies
        $\to$ best coloring with copies (3 seeds): density 69.8 $\to$ 92.6, cut\_ratio 80.4
        $\to$ 90.0. On ppi\_bp, coreness and component there was no gain.
  \item \textbf{Sibling edges do nothing.} With or without them, results differ by less than
        1 point on every dataset.
  \item \textbf{GLASS's training wins on most datasets.} 6 of the 8 new best setups use it.
        In step 1 it was ahead by 8 points on coreness and 4 on density (validation).
  \item \textbf{Aggregation and pooling mattered more than tuning.} Step 2 raised hpo\_metab's
        validation score from 62 to 67. Step 3's grid added at most 2.5 points.
  \item \textbf{Validation does not predict test well.} On hpo\_metab the new best scores 69 on
        validation but 61 on test. On cut\_ratio and density the previous model had the higher
        validation score (97.9 vs 95.0 and 97.9 vs 97.3), so picking by validation over all four
        models would have kept the previous one there.
\end{itemize}
